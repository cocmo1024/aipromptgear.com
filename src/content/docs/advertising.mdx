AI Prompt Gear is being structured to support future advertising and sponsorship placements, especially on high-intent reference pages where readers are actively evaluating workflow, tooling, and infrastructure decisions.

## Current status

Advertising placements are currently reserved as part of the site layout and information architecture. Their presence does not mean paid advertising is live on every page at this time.

The site currently prioritizes content quality, clear navigation, and policy readiness before commercial density. Placeholder planning, when visible in development or internal previews, should not be confused with live paid inventory. Public pages should remain readable and useful even when no advertiser is present.

## Ad readiness review table

| Review area | Acceptable state | Reject or revise when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page value | The article, hub, or policy page stands on its own without ads. | The page exists mainly to host placements. |
| Placement | Ads are visually separate from navigation, tables, and source notes. | A reader could mistake an ad for editorial guidance. |
| Density | Advertising does not crowd the first screen or interrupt core reading paths. | The page feels built around ad inventory rather than answers. |
| Commercial influence | Sponsored or affiliate relationships are disclosed where relevant. | A placement changes editorial conclusions without disclosure. |
| User trust | Contact, privacy, terms, disclaimer, and editorial pages remain easy to find. | Policy pages are hidden, vague, or inconsistent with site behavior. |

## Commercial placement philosophy

The site is designed around named placement zones so that commercial inventory can be managed consistently as coverage expands. This avoids manually placing ads page by page once the site contains a larger number of categories and reference pages.

The placement model should follow three rules:

- ads or sponsor messages must be visually separated from editorial guidance;
- sponsored presence must not determine which vendor, tool, model, or workflow is recommended;
- pages must continue to answer the reader's task without requiring a click on an advertiser.

## Intended placement types

- Right-rail sponsor placements on documentation pages.
- Footer band placements on deeper reference pages.
- Category-appropriate sponsor inventory on pages with strong research or buying intent.

## Commercial fit

The best future fit is likely to come from:

- Prompt workflow infrastructure.
- Model operations tooling.
- Evaluation and observability software.
- AI workflow platforms or related technical services.

## Poor commercial fit

The following requests are unlikely to fit the site:

- paid links inserted into unrelated pages;
- ads that imitate navigation, tables, buttons, or editorial recommendations;
- offers that require exaggerated claims about model performance, earnings, or guaranteed results;
- placements around prohibited, misleading, or low-quality products;
- campaigns that would make a page less useful for the reader's decision.

## Review before acceptance

Future advertising or sponsorship requests should be reviewed for audience fit, policy fit, placement clarity, and disclosure needs. A relevant sponsor may be appropriate on a comparison or workflow page, but that does not mean the sponsor controls the comparison, the inclusion criteria, or the conclusion. If a commercial relationship creates a conflict that cannot be disclosed clearly, the placement should be rejected.

## Transparency

Paid placements should be clearly separated from editorial content and should not silently change how products, vendors, or workflows are evaluated.
