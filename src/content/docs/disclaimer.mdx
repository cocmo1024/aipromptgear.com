AI Prompt Gear publishes informational and editorial content only. Nothing on this site should be interpreted as legal, financial, regulatory, compliance, cybersecurity, employment, or other professional advice.

The site is intended to help readers frame decisions and ask better questions about AI workflows. It cannot replace internal review, vendor due diligence, legal counsel, security assessment, procurement review, or production testing.

## Decision responsibility

Readers remain responsible for validating any workflow, tool, vendor, model, API, or implementation decision in their own environment. Suitability depends on internal policy, security requirements, legal obligations, budget, and operating context.

For high-impact decisions, readers should keep their own evidence record. That may include provider documentation, pricing pages, security statements, contract terms, incident history, logs, eval results, data-flow diagrams, approval records, and human review notes.

## No guarantees

The site may discuss tools, platforms, APIs, workflow patterns, or evaluation methods that change over time. Accuracy and relevance may shift as providers update their products, pricing, model behavior, or technical documentation.

Pages are reviewed on a cadence that reflects expected change risk, but review dates are not a guarantee that every external detail is still current. Market-sensitive pages, pricing-sensitive pages, and model-specific pages should be checked against current official sources before use.

## Reader verification checklist

Before acting on a page, readers should compare the page against the current primary source for the topic. For vendor pages, that usually means official documentation, pricing pages, model cards, status pages, security pages, and release notes. For operational pages, it may mean internal policies, escalation paths, audit requirements, incident response procedures, and production logs.

If a page includes a template, checklist, prompt, scorecard, or workflow, treat it as a starting point for local adaptation. The site cannot know a reader's data sensitivity, approval workflow, risk tolerance, customer obligations, or compliance environment. A useful page should make the next decision clearer, but it should not be the only evidence used for that decision.

| Page element | What it can support | What it cannot replace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision guides | Framing tradeoffs, risks, and next questions | Legal, procurement, security, or compliance approval |
| Prompt templates | A starting brief and evaluation checklist | Rights review, brand approval, or production QA |
| Tool comparisons | A way to structure vendor evaluation | Contract review or current pricing verification |
| Market signals | A map from current events to durable decisions | A live news feed or investment recommendation |
| Policy pages | Site transparency and reader expectations | Professional advice for a reader's organization |

## Editorial references

Mentions of vendors, products, services, or brands do not by themselves imply endorsement, partnership, or recommendation unless explicitly disclosed otherwise.

Comparisons are editorial tools for reasoning about fit. They are not exhaustive procurement reports. A vendor that is not mentioned may still be appropriate for a reader's situation, and a vendor that is mentioned may still be unsuitable under a particular contract, policy, budget, or technical constraint.

## Commercial relationships

The site may later contain advertising, sponsorship placements, or affiliate links. When that happens, those relationships should be disclosed through dedicated policy pages and contextual labels where appropriate.

Commercial disclosure does not remove the reader's need to evaluate a product independently. Advertising, sponsorship, or affiliate availability should not be treated as evidence that a vendor is approved for a specific operational use.
